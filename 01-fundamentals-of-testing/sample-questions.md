# Short Explanations for the Trickiest Questions

- **Q1:** Testing aims to reduce risk and build confidence, not prove that no defects exist.
- **Q2 and Q14:** Repeatedly running the same tests can stop uncovering new defects. Reviewing and varying tests can help.
- **Q3:** The SDLC, product risks, and regulatory requirements can significantly influence the test process. The supplied answer is **B**.
- **Q7:** An error made by a person can introduce a defect into a work product, which may cause a failure when executed.
- **Q9:** Passing tests provide evidence about quality, but they do not prove that all defects are absent.
- **Q12:** A product can satisfy specified requirements but still fail to meet users' actual needs.
- **Q17 vs Q18:** Confirmation testing checks a specific fix; regression testing checks for unintended consequences of changes.
- **Q23:** The whole-team approach means shared responsibility for quality, not that every person must perform every task.
- **Q25–Q28:** Remember the independence sequence: author → peer → separate internal team → external organization.
- **Q29–Q30:** Independence can introduce different perspectives, but poor collaboration can create communication problems.
- **Q33:** Defect concentration can guide additional testing, but prioritization should also consider risk and context.
